## What this repo is

Two fused lineages share one monorepo. The **live** one is the **agent harness**: retrieve a plan by topology, realise its geometry from a graph, judge it with sensors, and repair it with moves — all wrapping a locked deterministic core (design: workspace `docs/agent-harness-build-plan.md`). The **second**, `packages/floor_plate_design`, is the DfMA floor-plate generator + compliance checker (national-housing-program-adjacent); it dominates the repo by edge count but is not the harness. Harness work lives in `packages/floorplan_core`. The two are not fully separate: they meet at the **plate → unit handoff**. The plate generator subdivides a floor plate and hands each unit down as a *boundary + typed edges (`external` / `facing_corridor` / `shared_wall`) + a north bearing* (site orientation). That handoff is the unit harness's input — the unit harness never invents a unit's exposure or orientation, it receives them. The **facade-exposure typology** (below) is the shared vocabulary across the handoff, and the sensor/move/objective pattern is expected to recur at the plate level (a plate harness) when subdivision work resumes.

**Plate assembler (plan 2026-11-06; replaces `generate_initial_layout` and `generate_subdivision_response` for the demo chain, which stay in tree unused by it).** Why: on the three G0 plates neither route gave a core and units together (`tools/diag_plate_generator.py`). Brain: `packages/floor_plate_design/assembler.py`, pure functions, deterministic, no Flask. Principle: generate candidates, reject by hard rules, select by the style sheet (`floor-plate-kit/style/style-sheet.html` in the artefacts folder). Scope for 27 Nov: orthogonal outlines, one core, rectangular units. Interlocking and second cores come later. A non-orthogonal outline returns `status: "unsupported"`.

Input: `boundary_m`, `mix` (proportions over `1B1b`, `2B1b`, `2B2b`, `3B2b`), `street_edges`. The main entrance is not used on a typical floor. Unit count is derived from what fits, not requested.

Rules:
1. **Wings.** Enumerate the ways to cut the outline into rectangles at reflex vertices. Each decomposition is a candidate set.
2. **Access by depth.** For a wing of depth D, with hall `h` = 1.5 m and minimum unit depth `d` = 5.0 m: double-loaded if D ≥ 2d + h; single-loaded if D ≥ d + h, hall on either long side, preferring the side away from street frontage; otherwise through units with no hall, reached only at the wing's junction with the core or a hall.
3. **Core.** One stair + lift block of about 20 m², at least 2.8 m wide, sized to the depth of the strip it sits in, up to 6.5 m. Candidate positions: along each hall and at wing junctions and corners. Prefer positions off street frontage.
4. **Hall.** A connected strip from the core, ending at the last door it serves. Never longer than needed. End units run through past it.
5. **Units.** Rectangles cut across each strip, so party walls are straight. Limits per type `{min area, target area, min frontage}` are provisional: `1B1b` 36/50/4.5, `2B1b` 50/65/5.5, `2B2b` 55/72/5.5, `3B2b` 65/90/6.5. A unit stays between its minimum and 1.4 × target.
6. **Leftovers.** Area that no unit can take goes to commons when it adjoins them, else to `residual` (reported, drawn yellow). It is never silently added to a unit beyond rule 5.
7. **Hard rejects.** Anything outside the outline, overlaps, a unit below minimum area or depth, a unit with no door contact (≥ 0.9 m shared with commons), a unit with no facade (≥ 2.4 m on the outline), a disconnected hall.
8. **Selection, in order.** Smallest mix error (A6: within 1 unit per type); largest unit area in total; smallest commons share; shortest walk from core to farthest door; least residual; most even widths.
9. **Typed edges from real geometry.** Polygons get extra vertices where contact changes, so each edge has one type: `external` on the outline, `facing_corridor` only where it touches real commons, `shared_wall` otherwise. One `entrance_point` per unit, on its commons contact.
10. **Honest output.** If no candidate survives, return `status: "infeasible"` with the reject counts. Never change the mix silently; report requested and achieved.

Output uses the `FloorPlateState` vocabulary: `boundary`, `typology` (per wing), `cores`, `corridors`, `units` (`id`, `type`, `polygon`, `typed_edges`, `entrance_point`, `depth`, `area`), `residual`, plus `metrics` and `rejects`. Wiring: `POST /assemble_floor_plate`.

**Second pass (2026-11-06, from Camilo's review of `test-plate-001`). These amend the rules above and win where they differ.** Input adds `north_deg`.
- **Rule 3a, orientation.** North is the sun side (Victoria; `PARAMS["sun_bearing_deg"]` = 0, relative to true north). A facade's bearing comes from its outward normal and `north_deg`. The core avoids sun-side facades and prefers the opposite side.
- **Rule 3b, core block.** The block is a stair + lift zone plus a lobby, about 20 m² in total, drawn as two parts. Doors open off the lobby. The block may run the strip's full depth, up to 8 m, so no commons sliver is left behind it.
- **Rule 4a, hall.** The hall stops at its last door, and its length can be zero if the lobby reaches every door. The band beyond the hall goes to an adjoining unit.
- **Rule 5a, unit shape.** A unit is a rectangle, or an L with one step when it takes that band. Size limits still apply.
- **Rule 5b, types.** Types follow size: a larger type is never smaller than a smaller type in the same plate.
- **Rule 8a, selection order.** Mix error; least core frontage on sun-side facades; smallest commons share; shortest walk; unit areas closest to target (moved below commons and walk after review: closeness to target gave a 14 m hall on `test-plate-001`); most even widths; least residual; least commons on street frontage. Residual draws yellow.

As built, second pass (2026-11-06): one host wing carries core and hall, and other wings get no hall; the sun term counts the core only, so a hall can run along a sun-side facade; `allow_leftover` is False, so a piece too small for a unit rejects the candidate and residual is always 0; added hard rule `hall_to_facade_unneeded`; an L arm is at least 1.5 m (`min_band`). Dev panel starts hidden, toggle `#fp-dev-toggle`.

As built, first pass (2026-11-06), differing from the rules above: the core and halls sit in one host wing, and other wings get no hall (rule 3 candidates at junctions and corners are not all generated); off-street preference ranks before mix error (rule 8 puts mix first); a through unit's depth is measured from its door face; residual draws red dashed in the app, not yellow. Known defects seen on the G0 plates: commons slivers where the core depth cap is shorter than the strip; unit type not ordered by area; no regularity or repetition term beyond "most even widths". Tests: `tests/test_assembler.py`, `tools/e2e/plate_assembler.cjs`.

## The data structure everything hangs on — the plan / unit dict (v2.1.0)

`boundary` [[x,y],…] · `rooms` [{id, type/label, polygon}] · `edges` [{u,v,connection}] (room↔room adjacency) · `entrance` [x0,y0,x1,y1] · `windows`/`doors` · `retrieval` {dist, mirror, topo{…}} · and the **derived topology layer**: `room_edges` (room↔boundary, raycast), `node_pos` (room node in target frame), `flags` (sensor results). Retrieved vs derived vs editable matters: room↔room edges are retrieved, then reconciled after a move (see The two topologies); room↔boundary (`room_edges`/`node_pos`) is derived by `topo_signature`; the agent will edit only the spec, never geometry.

## Node graph (decision 2026-11-03)

The **node graph** is the primary graph; the room graph (`rooms` + `edges`) is legacy for consumers but stays the node graph's source (node ids carry the room id). It is stored as a **sidecar** keyed by plan id while the decomposition rules still move, and is baked into an enriched dataset copy (v2.2.0) when they freeze. The search index (`rplan_index_v5.npz`) is unchanged: queries carry no graph. The corpus node-type adjacency stats (`tools/corpus_node_adjacency_stats.py`) are the first consumer (2026-11-03). **Retrieval is the second (2026-11-03, on by default; `node_daylight: false` or env `NODE_DAYLIGHT=0` turns it off):** `/retrieve` loads the sidecar once (`server._node_graphs`, env `NODE_GRAPH_SIDECAR`) and passes `node_graphs` to `run_retrieval` → `topological_fitness(node_graph=)` → `_daylight_fit` → `_source_ring_rooms`, which assigns perimeter samples to node polygons: a room's circulation/storage nodes no longer count towards its daylight. Other topo terms stay room-level; the returned plan is unchanged. `tools/compare_node_daylight.py` compares both modes on the boundary library (top-5 unchanged on all 8 boundaries; 0–3 of 60 pool candidates rescored, all upward). Also 2026-11-03: `topo_signature._label` prefers the room's string label — `schema.ROOM_TYPE_MAP` (fitting-prototype) is off by one from type 6 (6 study, 7 storage, 9 entrance), which had made studies non-daylight and storage rooms transit on retrieved plans. Everything else still reads the room graph; migrating generation (one rectangle per node), the daylight re-rank (demand per node) and the corpus adjacency stats is the next work. RPlan has no open edges (connection 0 wall / 1 door only).

**Display (2026-11-04).** `run_retrieval` attaches `retrieval.node_graph` to each returned plan (`node_graph.display_graph`: circulation split into rectangular arms, a label point `pos` inside each node; about 6 ms per plan; same switch as `node_daylight`). `drawTopologyGraph` (`canvas-renderer.js`, cards and main canvas) draws it when present: tinted primary and arm polygons, arms/storage/nooks as small squares (`Hw`/`St`/`Nk`), edges wall (dashed), door (solid dark), open/block (solid teal). An orange line joins `entry_node` to the entrance. **Room ids stay the key for selection, sensor flags and room↔boundary connectors**: each room has one anchor node (whole/primary, else largest), so swap still works on rooms and sees no node ids. A swap sets `retrieval.node_graph` undefined, since it describes the unswapped geometry, and the graph falls back to the room graph; reset restores it. Without the graph (sidecar off, no entry) the room graph is drawn. Nothing else in the frontend reads node ids yet.

## Node-level generation (plan + first build, 2026-11-03)

**Why.** The generator placed one rectangle per room. RPlan living rooms are mostly L/T: a primary plus a circulation band that carries the doors to the private rooms (RPlan 26135: all four doors land on the living room's circulation; distribution ratio 1.0). One rectangle per room loses the band; other rooms take its place, doors are dropped, rooms become unreachable. Circulation is therefore modelled as rooms open to the living area — the arrangement designers drew.

**Input — `node_graph.expand_plan(plan, node_graph)`.** Every node becomes a room (id = node id, `room_id` = source room). Circulation regions are split into **rectangular arms** (`r_2/circ0a`, `r_2/circ0b`, label `Hallway`), joined by open edges (connection 2, `separation: open`) to each other and to the primary; each door re-attaches to the arm it faces. Storage blocks become `Storage`; nooks keep the room label. Invariant: **every generated node is a rectangle.**

**Size rule — the open-plan space (decision 2026-11-03).** A room's minimum applies to its group (ids sharing the prefix before `/`). Generator (`spec_from_plan`): the primary keeps `min_edge` and takes its area share of `min_area`; hallway arms keep the hallway width (0.9 m) and no area; nooks/storage carry no demand. D24 (`check_min_room_size`): the area test runs on the union of the group, the core test on the primary; arms get the width test only.

**Placement.** The default (node-driven) placement does not enforce doors — that is what dropped the bedroom door on 26135, with or without nodes. CP-SAT (`core_placement: "cpsat" | "cpsat_dim"`) makes door/open touches hard, so it is the placement node generation is gated with. CP-SAT changes (2026-11-03): **conservative snapping** — the allowed bbox rounds inward, forbidden cells are cut on a shared floor/ceil grid (non-overlapping) and kept unless wholly inside, boundary edges round inward — so rooms never overhang the true boundary (this removed the D24 near misses, e.g. 2373 mm against 2400); hallway nodes get aspect ≤ 6 (others 2.5).

**Entrance (2026-11-04).** The entrance is read from the labelled door, not from proximity: the node graph's edge to `exterior` gives `node_graph.entry_node`; `entry_room_of(plan)` sets `spec["entry_room"]` (rooms mode: that node's source room; `topo["entry_room"]`, the nearest room, is only the fallback, since a bathroom beside the door would win). Generation honours it in every placement. Default: step 2b gives the entry room the grid cell in front of the entrance, first (`_entrance_cell`, pseudo-edge `"entrance"`, `report.boundary_contacts.entrance.met`); the claim can fail when a donor cannot spare the cell (`test_boundary_004` rank 3). CP-SAT and `cpsat_dim`: `_add_entrance_front`, hard, covers a door's width around the point clipped to the edge; free CP-SAT releases it only when no layout exists (`relaxation: "no_entrance"`). `entrance_valid` passes any transit room, so it cannot tell a hallway from the primary. The generated plan's `room_edges` live in the frontend's `generatedRoomEdges`, never on `retrieval.room_edges`.

**Connections stay as the source drew them (decision 2026-11-03).** RPlan records dining–living and kitchen–dining as doors (it has no open type). Generation keeps them as doors; opening them is a Stage-3 move (SetConnection), not a default. Daylight/size failures that an opening would resolve are Stage-3 input, not generation errors. Arms of one circulation region are interchangeable for doors: `reconcile_edges` moves a door to a sibling arm that touches the room instead of dropping it.

**Wiring.** `/generate` and `/moves/propose` take `expand_nodes` (request) or env `NODE_GENERATE` (default `0` = off). Since 2026-11-04 the app sends it from the stage 0 checkbox "generate from node graph", on by default, on the user's call that the node graph is primary; the 2026-11-03 gate result (97/126 against 103–104) stands and the server default stays off. A swap selects rooms: `node_graph.anchor_node` maps each id to its whole/primary node. Hallways draw amber in the generated overlay.

**Gate.** `tools/diag_placement_topology.py` runs off / cpsat / cpsat_dim × rooms / nodes over 18 plans (6 boundaries × top 3); node runs are collapsed to source rooms before lost-edge and order checks. Adoption: a nodes+placement pair at least matches the best rooms run on sensors passed, with no more reachability, entrance or lost door/open failures.

**Open.** Generation has no maximum for a hallway: `min_edge` 0.9 m only, so a hallway node can take a room's area (51270 cpsat: 4.90 × 4.29 m) and the sensors pass it. CP-SAT is non-deterministic (8 workers, 5 s): the same request can score 6/7 or 7/7 — gates need a fixed seed / single worker. Node ids (`r_2/circ0a`) in the frontend, `swap`, `reconcile_edges` and SetConnection are untested. The adjacency-informed `pair_weights` and the zoning table are not yet node-aware.

## The Spec (schema agreed and implemented 2026-11-04: `packages/floorplan_core/spec.py`)

**What it is.** The Spec is the formal description of a floor plan. Fed to the Generator, it produces the plan. Moves edit only the Spec. `spec.py` holds `FIELDS` (this table), `describe`, `fit`, `build_options`, `spec_problems` and `validate_spec`; `generate()` validates at entry and raises ValueError. Flat dict (decision 2026-11-04).

**Describe, then fit (2026-11-04).** `describe(plan)` is the plan's own Spec: its boundary, entrance, a position inside each room, the boundary edges it really fronts. `fit(source_spec, boundary, ...)` replaces the given fields with the input (`boundary`, `edge_types`, `entrance`, `mm_per_unit`) and re-derives `pos` and `boundary_demands` from the topology layer; identity, labels, adjacencies, demands and `entry_room` stay. `tools/diag_round_trip.py` feeds `describe(plan)` (and `fit` onto the plan's own boundary) to the Generator and compares with the plan, which measures generator loss with no input boundary involved. 300 plans, placement off: median room overlap 0.64, door or open edge lost in 194 plans (rooms) and 136 (nodes). 30 plans, cpsat: overlap 0.42 (rooms) and 0.55 (nodes), doors lost in 4 and 8. Identity fit adds little. Results: `data/outputs/diagnostics/round_trip_2026-11-04.json`.

**Contract.** `validate_spec(spec)` rejects a malformed description. `generate(spec, options) -> plan` realises it. `run_sensors(plan, ...)` judges it. The Generator promises: (1) same Spec + options -> same plan; (2) the Spec is not changed; (3) every node yields a room with the same id and label, or is reported dropped; (4) rooms stay inside the boundary and do not overlap; (5) every Spec statement is realised or listed unmet. It never hides a failure and never judges one: `report` says what could not be realised, sensors say what is wrong by the norms. Sensors read limits from `MINIMUM_SIZES`, not the Spec, so no move can pass a sensor by lowering a demand. Today: (1) fails under CP-SAT (8 workers; `options` carries `cpsat_seed`, `cpsat_workers`, `cpsat_time_s`, current values kept); (2) tested (`tests/test_spec.py`); (4) partly reported; (5) incomplete for sizes.

**Strength.** must = the plan is not coherent without it (`feasible: false`); should = attempted, misses listed in `report`; guide = steers the solve, cannot be missed.

| `precedence` | Which of two spaces wins a contested area | `[[winner, loser]]` | design | should | SetPrecedence |

**Moved to `options`:** `core_placement`, `core_block`, `area_lift`, `source_rooms`, `pair_weights`, solver seed and workers. `_claim_choice` becomes an internal Generator argument.

**Invariants (`validate_spec`).** Every id in `demands`, `adjacencies`, `boundary_demands`, `entry_room`, `precedence` exists in `nodes`. One edge type per boundary edge; edge indices in range. `connection` in 0, 1, 2. Each minimum <= its maximum. `entrance` within 1% of the boundary's bbox diagonal, or None (the Generator self-test needs None). `area_frac` totals 1 within 0.02. `build_spec` drops adjacencies with an unknown endpoint and self-loops. Plan edges keep `separation` for the sensors; Spec adjacencies hold `connection` only. The min_area lift still uses `ASPECT_MAX` 2.5 in `generate.py`, to keep its numbers.

**Doors and windows (decision 2026-11-04).** Derived, not stated in the Spec: a parametric, deterministic function in the Generator, its parameters in `options`, its output in the plan's `doors` / `windows`. Not yet built.

**Swap (decision 2026-11-04).** A label exchange. Node ids are fixed slots; connections and boundary contacts belong to the slot, so they stay. `label` and all `demands` follow the function: topology is fixed, sizes re-solve. `pos` is untouched. Whole rooms only, for now: a primary and its arms are sized as one space with one function, so swapping a primary would need groups redefined. Will replace the position swap, still live (`reassign`, the `arrange` route through `swap.py`, `/swap_nodes`, `node_graph.anchor_node`). Not yet built.

**Compound moves (decision 2026-11-04).** A connection made wrong by a swap is not a reason to forbid the swap. Sensors flag it, and a secondary move can repair it: SetConnection turns an inherited open edge into a door; SlideEntrance moves the entrance to a hallway on a corridor edge. Such a sequence is viable but ranks below one that needs no repair. So move guards stay few, and the harness must evaluate sequences: a one-step greedy search would reject these swaps.

## The two topologies

**room↔room** — from the plan's `edges` (adjacency/door graph); at retrieval, `filter_corner_adjacencies` (topo_signature) drops any source edge whose two room polygons meet only at a corner (shared boundary length ≤ tol) — a point contact is not an adjacency, so it never enters the drawn graph, generation, or reachability. Fixed at retrieval, but **reconciled after any move** (`reconcile_edges` in `rectangular_arrange`, applied by both `swap` and `generate`): connections (door/open) follow room identity where still realised-adjacent, walls are re-derived from the realised geometry, and a connection whose rooms no longer touch is reported as a **dropped connection** — so a move never leaves stale identity-keyed edges for the drawn graph or the sensors to read. **room↔boundary** — derived geometrically by raycast in `topo_signature` (`_raycast_room_edges`), never from the dataset's window annotations. For each exterior wall a ray is cast from the **room node** (robust: a node-origin ray reaches an edge even on a non-convex boundary) to find the target edge; the contact is kept only if (a) the wall's placed midpoint is within **PROX_TOL** of that edge — a *proximity* test that keeps a genuine contact whose ray crosses a neighbour (LR→corridor with the bathroom in between) while dropping a phantom whose wall sits deep interior (bathroom→north) — and (b) its realised width clears the **edge-type floor**: `WINDOW_MIN_WIDTH_M` 0.8 m on `external` edges (a window; project choice 2026-10-30, below the BADS 1.2 m) or `DOOR_MIN_M` 0.9 m on `facing_corridor` edges (a door). Each contact carries `width_m`; the daylight sensor (D26) tests that physical width, not a fraction. The daylight/access/capacity sensors read both derived layers, so these guards correct the scores as well as the drawing.

**room↔boundary on generated geometry (closed 2026-10-29; exact since 2026-10-30).** `room_edges` was computed on the *retrieved* plan and reused for `/generate`, but the node-driven solver re-tiles the boundary, so which room fronts an edge changes (a room drawn off the corridor still carried the source's corridor contact — the LR→west phantom). `/generate` now re-derives `room_edges` from the generated polygons via `_raycast_room_edges(..., in_target_space=True)`: the polygons and nodes are already in target space, so the display transform is identity and the plan's own boundary is the target. The same proximity test + edge-type width floor apply. Both the drawn overlay and the Stage-1 sensors now read realised frontage. **Since 2026-10-30 `/generate` uses `exact_room_edges` instead of the raycast:** generated rooms already sit in the target, so a contact is a room wall lying along a boundary edge — overlap intervals merged per edge, `width_m` = the widest run. The raycast kept one hit per ray and lost contacts (a living room fronting a 2.6 m window was reported on its 1.6 m one). `/retrieve` and `/topo_check` keep the raycast (`in_target_space=False`). Same output shape, same floors.

## Main action, end to end

Draw boundary+entrance+requirements → `/retrieve` → `run_retrieval` (npz search → pool of 60 → `topological_fitness` re-rank, attaches `node_pos`+`room_edges`) → winner drawn as a topology graph → `/topo_check` (`topo_sensors.run_sensors`) → select two nodes → `/swap_nodes` (`swap` → `rectangular_arrange.arrange` re-realises geometry) → re-check sensors.

## Agent harness — the live build (Stages 0–1 built; Stage 2 started)

The forward design (workspace `docs/agent-harness-build-plan.md`): a fixed Observe → propose → rollout → select → escalate loop over two open registries (Sensors, Moves) with fixed contracts, wrapping a **locked Generator**.

**Stage 1 — BUILT (2026-10-26):** `packages/floorplan_core/topo_sensors.py` migrated to the sensor contract — each sensor has `kind: hard|soft` and `evaluate→{ok, failing_rooms, margin}`; `run_sensors` returns `{summary{passed, total, gates_pass, soft_score}, checks}`. Eight sensors: reachability / access_typed / entrance_valid (topology, hard), **daylight (D26), room_depth (D25), min_room_size (D24)**, boundary_capacity (geometric, hard), node_in_room (soft). Daylight and depth are judged per **open-plan component** (connected components over `open` edges): an unlit non-private room passes only when open-connected to a lit one — the compliant form of borrowed light, the open/closed-kitchen decision. **An edge is open only when it says so** (`separation: open` or `connection: 2`); the earlier inference that a door between two common rooms is open plan was dropped 2026-10-30 (it let a windowless kitchen borrow light through a door and measured depth across doors). Edges carry `connection` 0 wall / 1 door / 2 open, plus an authoritative `separation` written by SetConnection; open edges are passable. **Layers (2026-10-30):** `run_sensors(layer=)` — `"topology"` (retrieved plan: `/retrieve` flags, `/topo_check`) skips `GEOMETRIC_SENSORS` (room_depth, min_room_size, node_in_room), whose polygons are still the source plan's; `"geometry"` (generated plan) skips `TOPOLOGY_ONLY_SENSORS` (boundary_capacity — a pre-geometry feasibility question that pinned each daylight room's min width to one edge). **D24 (2026-10-30)** measures the usable footprint, not the bbox: core = a `min_edge` square must fit; area = the room's usable area (reachable by a `USABLE_WIDTH_MM` 900 square) must meet `min_area`; reports pinched rooms and area below min width. **D25 (2026-10-30)** is wall by wall: a wall opposes a window when parallel and facing it; its depth is the distance to its nearest opposing window; every such wall must be within the limit; walls on a window line are skipped (dual aspect). Thresholds are grounded in the workspace doc `projects/project-44/docs/bads-sensor-model.md` (Better Apartment Design Standards, Victoria). Scored on real `/generate` output and shown in the in-app "stage 1 · sensors" panel. The sensors run on generated geometry, so `topo_signature._label` now **canonicalizes** label spellings (int / `"LivingRoom"` / `"living_room"`) to the category vocabulary — without it, generated-plan rooms mis-classified silently. Sensor `detail` strings name rooms one way, `r_2 (kitchen)` (`_nm`/`_nms`); `failing_rooms` stays bare ids. `access_typed` stays strict (doors, not adjacency) and returns `door_candidates` `[[room, provider]]` — wall-adjacent transit rooms with a shared wall ≥ 0.9 m — the exact-effect input for SetConnection (2026-10-30).

**Stage 0 — BUILT (2026-10-24):** `packages/floorplan_core/generate.py` carries the `Spec`/`Plan` seam (`spec_from_plan`, `generate`, `reassign`) and the node-driven solver — room polygons from node positions + boundary: node-seeded grid → **containing-cell seeding** + nearest-node fill → **boundary-contact repair (step 2b)** → **area-weighted re-dimension** (each room sized toward its source `area_frac`, a D1 dimensional demand) → clip → **thin-arm carve (step 4b)**. Replaces `arrange()`'s cell-from-source-bbox grid. **Step 2b (added 2026-10-29) makes topology drive geometry:** the Spec carries `boundary_demands` (`{room_id: [target_edge_index]}` — which target edge each room should front, from the topo-rank ring alignment, threaded in by `/generate`), and `_repair_boundary_contacts` transfers border cells so a room reaches a demanded edge it would not own by node proximity alone (the LR→west case: LR's node is central, so the west column goes to the bathroom; 2b gives LR a contiguous west cell). A transfer commits only if every donor stays non-empty, 4-connected, and keeps its own fronts; it tries candidate edge cells shortest-first and commits the first valid one. **Step 4b (added 2026-10-29) catches what a whole-cell transfer cannot:** when the demanded edge is over-subscribed and the room is interior (the LR→west case — four rooms want the west side, the column holds three), `_carve_arm` gives the room a **thin L-shaped arm** reaching the edge, carved from the rooms it crosses (the source's thin reach a rectangular partition can't make). The arm width clears the edge's door/window floor (`×1.05`); a carve commits only if every donor stays non-empty, single-piece, and keeps its own demanded fronts. Satisfied-by-arm demands move to `report.boundary_contacts.arms`. The guarantee is conditional — a demand with no feasible transfer or carve is reported in `report.boundary_contacts.unmet`, not forced. Exposed at `/generate`; in-app visual test is the "stage 0 · generate from nodes" sidebar panel (generate / generate+swap / reset), redrawing the generated rooms over the topology nodes. Known limits, reported not hidden: on a **non-convex (notched) boundary** a node's grid column can cross the notch, so the clipped room may not contain its node — `report.node_relocated` counts these and the display marker is moved to the room interior; and exact per-room areas can't be hit on a shared grid (`ncol+nrow` DOF < n rooms), so `report.area_mae` measures the residual (~2% on convex, ~6% with notches). Min room size remains a Stage-1 sensor. A true Voronoi realiser would guarantee node-in-cell but at the cost of non-rectangular rooms (deferred). ARAP is retired as the realiser. Next: Stage 1 (sensor interface + min-size + the facade-exposure context of §6b). Requirement held: every stage gets an in-app visual test (extend the topology-graph canvas + sensor-flags panel). **Min sizes (2026-10-30):** `spec_from_plan` labels rooms via `topo_signature._label` (canonical), so `MINIMUM_SIZES`/`FLEX` resolve; `/generate` defaults `mm_per_unit` to `SCALE` (the frontend does not send it). Before this, step 3 enforced no minimum. `report.rooms` carries area provenance — `area_rect` (after step 3, before clip), `area_clip`, `arm_donor_to` — and `_carve_arm` returns its donors. **Notch-aligned grid (2026-10-30):** step 1 pins the boundary's vertex coordinates as grid lines (`_grid_lines(..., fixed=)`); cells whose centre lies outside the boundary get no owner, and step 3 (`_solve_positions(..., fixed=)`) keeps pinned lines in place and moves only the node cuts, so on an orthogonal boundary the clip removes nothing. `PIN_MIN_SEG_FACTOR` (default 0.0 = pin every vertex) can skip short jogs; measured worse. `report.grid_lines` {xs, ys, pinned_x, pinned_y, cells[i, j, owner|None]} feeds the canvas grid drawn under the generated rooms. **`min_area` lift (2026-10-30):** after a feasible step-3 solve, the worst-off room's per-axis minimum is raised (both axes ×√f, else one axis ×f) and re-solved; a trial is kept only if both axes stay feasible, the room's aspect stays ≤ `ASPECT_MAX` 2.5 (or its prior ratio), and no room that met `min_area` falls below it. Reported in `debug_steps[3_resolve]` (`area_lifted`, `area_unmet`) and `report.rooms[].area_lift`; `spec["area_lift"]=False` turns it off (the app toggle). Known limits: the lift targets cell area, which counts arms the usable-footprint D24 does not; non-orthogonal edges still clip.

**Step 0 — placement modes (2026-11-02, uncommitted, default off):** `spec["core_placement"]` selects how room blocks are placed before the grid: `False`/`"off"` (node-driven, as above), `True`/`"greedy"` (`core_placement.place_cores` — graph-walk order, door/open pairs need ≥ 0.9 m shared core wall), `"cpsat"` (`layout_cpsat.solve_layout` — one rectangle per room on a 100 mm grid; min_edge/min_area/aspect, in-boundary NoOverlap2D and door/open touch hard; boundary fronts hard then soft; objective = covered area − node L1 + `pair_weights` wall term; 5 s / 8 workers), `"cpsat_dim"` (`solve_layout_dim` — as cpsat, plus the source arrangement from `topo_signature.map_rooms_to_target`: door sides, centre order and the entry room fronting the entrance hard, walls soft). All return `{cores, nodes, report}`; generate pins the rectangles as grid lines and seeds their cells. Step 2b refuses any transfer out of another room's placed core (`core_cells` guard). Gate over 18 plans (`tools/diag_placement_topology.py`): sensors passed off 74 / cpsat 93 / cpsat_dim 85 of 126; cpsat rearranges plans (entrance into a bedroom on `005b`); cpsat_dim fails its gate — one rectangle per room cannot hold L/T source arrangements. ## Gates (2026-11-04)

Command: `.venv/bin/python tools/gate_all.py` (about 7 min). `--fast` skips placement, round_trip_dev and e2e. `--stage NAME`, `--change FILE`, `--update-baseline --reason "..."`. Report: `data/outputs/gates/report_<date>_<hash>.md` and `.json`. Exit 1 on any FAIL.

Stages: `unit` (pytest), `selftest` (generate.py), `moves` (SetConnection and SetPrecedence gates, mismatches 0), `determinism` (same Spec + options give a byte-identical plan, 3 plans, cpsat and cpsat_dim, in two processes), `placement` (18 plans, 6 runs), `round_trip_dev` (300 dev plans, 30 with cpsat), `e2e` (`tools/e2e/app_flow.cjs`: own backend on 5012, own Vite on 5183, Playwright; it FAILS if Playwright is absent). `round_trip_holdout` runs only with `--stage round_trip_holdout`: totals, no verdict, logged in `holdout_log.md`.

Settings: 1 CP-SAT worker, default seed, `cpsat_det_time` 1.0 (`spec.build_options`; app default stays None), `PYTHONHASHSEED=0` (Generator output depends on string hashing across processes). Plan sets: `data/outputs/gates/plan_sets.json` (dev 300, holdout 300, fixed seed, disjoint from each other and from `GATE_IDS`).

Rule modes (`tools/gate_rules.py`). Default: every tracked metric and every per-plan `sensors_ok` / `passed` must equal the baseline; any difference fails. `--change FILE` (JSON in `data/outputs/gates/changes/`, `written_before_code: true`): declared `must_improve` and `may_move` metrics; an undeclared metric must not get worse; an undeclared improvement is reported, not failed. Baselines: `data/outputs/gates/baselines/main.json` (records the git hash), changed only by `--update-baseline --reason`, each change appended to `baseline_log.md`.

The hold-out is not used for tuning. Read its totals; do not change code to move them. A gate pass does not replace looking: the report lists screenshots under "View by hand" (`data/outputs/gates/shots/<hash>/`).
